Section III — The 200 Steps

The Core Curriculum

Four phases of 50 steps each. The phases are not strictly sequential — you can be working on a Phase 3 step while still practicing a Phase 1 anchor. But there is a general direction: soil before training, training before unraveling, unraveling before radiating.

Phase 1: Cultivating the Soil (Steps 1–50)

The foundation work. Build the container that will hold everything else.

StepPracticeAnchor
1Radical honestyTell the truth about one small thing today.
2Self-compassionSpeak to yourself the way you would speak to a child.
3Daily meditationSit for 20 minutes. Focus on the breath.
4Sleep hygieneSame bedtime, same wake time, every day for a week.
5HydrationOne liter of water before noon.
6Walking20 minutes of bilateral movement.
7One thing at a timePick one task. Close everything else.
8Saying noDecline one request today that you would have said yes to.
9Reading20 pages of something that isn't news.
10Setting an intentionDecide the one thing today is for.

The principle: you cannot build on what you haven't leveled. Phase 1 is leveling. Don't skip it because it feels too simple. The simplicity is the point.

Phase 2: Training the Mind (Steps 51–100)

Once the soil is established, the mind becomes the practice.

StepPracticeAnchor
51VipassanaObserve the changing nature of thoughts without grasping.
52EquanimityStay balanced when circumstances change.
53Non-attachmentLet go of one specific expectation for an outcome.
54Witness practiceWatch the mind think without participating.
55NotingLabel experience as it arises: "sound, sound, thought, sensation."
56Choice-point awarenessNotice the moment before a reaction.
57Energy hygieneAudit what you consume in a day — input is also practice.
58Gratitude without comparisonBe grateful without ranking the gifts.
59Difficult conversationsHave one you've been avoiding.
60RepairApologize for one thing you've let sit.

The principle: the mind is a tool, not an identity. Phase 2 builds the ability to use the mind without being used by it.

Phase 3: Unraveling the Illusion (Steps 101–150)

The turning point. What was built in Phase 1 is gently dismantled in Phase 3.

StepPracticeAnchor
101The Three MarksAnicca (impermanence), Dukkha (suffering), Anatta (non-self).
102EmptyingRecognize the interconnectedness of all things.
103Self-inquiryConstantly ask: "Who is the 'I' that is experiencing this?"
104Death contemplationSit with the certainty of your own ending.
105The witness and the witnessedNotice that the observer is also observed.
106Letting go of viewsHold a position lightly. Be ready to revise it.
107The ungraspable mindThoughts arise without a thinker behind them.
108Direct pointingWhat is awareness right now, before any concept of it?
109Suffering as teacherTreat pain as information, not as punishment.
110Identity experimentsNotice which "selves" you defend. Try not defending them.

The principle: the self you built is not the self you are. Phase 3 is the careful deconstruction of that misconception. It is not nihilism. It is precision.

Phase 4: Radiating Awareness (Steps 151–200)

What remains when the illusion is seen through is not nothing. It is everything, recognized.

StepPracticeAnchor
151ServiceDedicate your life to the benefit of all beings.
152AweMaintain a Beginner's Mind toward the universe.
153UnityLive in the realization that there is no "other."
154Compassion without boundaryExtend care beyond your in-group.
155Forgiveness as practiceForgive the unforgivable — not for them, for you.
156Generosity as identityGive without tracking the return.
157Work as offeringWhatever your hands find to do, do it as practice.
158SpaciousnessMake room for others to fail, grow, and be.
159Unconditional presenceBe fully here for whoever is in front of you.
160The smile that precedes contentLet the face settle into ease before the mind settles into meaning.

The principle: enlightenment is not a state. It is what is left when you stop preventing it. Phase 4 is the return to ordinary life, with full awareness.

The sequence is a heuristic, not a law

You will find yourself returning to Phase 1 in your 30s, your 50s, your 70s. Each life-event that disrupts your foundations sends you back to soil-work. This is not regression. This is how the curriculum actually works when lived. Treat the phases as seasons, not semesters.

— Kai Djuric, Belgrade
