01 — Orientation

Three libraries live in one directory. Only two ship.

Before you plan against this, know which layer you are reading. They are built with different intents, and conflating them is the fastest way to misuse the set.

SHIPS 28

03 — Anatomy

Eight sections, one argument.

Every workflow persona uses the same template. Learn it once and you can scan all 28 in minutes — the useful fields are "a typical session" and "what would make them leave."

slug + surfacesThe route key and which of the 11 surfaces the persona genuinely touches. A 3+ minimum, enforced by the coverage matrix.
who they areSecond person, with a toolbelt — the software they already pay for and the habit that predates the engine.
what they wantThe job to be done, stated as a workflow with an obvious end — a client approval, a lecture, an archive drop.
why each surfaceA numbered list of surfaces with the reason attached. This is where route names, presets and transitions appear.
typical session90–180 minutes, step by step, always ending in an "almost quit at step N" moment. That near-abandonment is the most valuable line in the file.
pay forScope and pricing shape, plus the thing they explicitly refuse to pay for. One-time purchase vs. subscription is a real signal.
leaveThe single most actionable field. A refusal, and the concession they will tolerate forever.
quoteOne sentence in their voice that names the surfaces they trust. Read all 28 quotes in a row and you get the product thesis.

04 — Coverage

Which surfaces carry the most weight.

Count of the 28 workflow personas touching each surface. For a planner this is a blast-radius map: change the top two and you have changed the library.

The two poles

Presets (22) and the recorder (20) are the most-touched surfaces — and the two with the cleanest mental models (an anchor map, a WebM file). The engine core sits just behind at 20: connective tissue almost every workflow passes through, rarely anyone's primary surface.

The thin edge

Projects/auth is touched by only 6 personas — the smallest set, with the strongest signal: every one of them trusts the magic-link login specifically, and none would accept OAuth as a substitute.

The quiet signal

The 14-FX pipeline is touched by 10 personas — and in 7 of those, chroma is the exact uniform they push. It is the single most-named specific feature in the whole library.

07 — Playbook

How to actually use this in a project.

STEP 01

Name the surface before the feature

Write the spec against ENG, VER, FX, TX, AR, SB, REC, PST, AAP, LIB or AUD. If a feature does not land on a surface, no persona in this library can validate it.

STEP 02

Pull everyone who touches it

Use the coverage matrix as the invite list. Changing presets means 22 stakeholders. Changing auth means 6 — but all six are immovable on the mechanism.

STEP 03

Read the session, not the bio

"A typical session" tells you where the workflow breaks today — usually the almost quit line. That is your real acceptance test, and it is usually not a crash.

STEP 04

Treat "leave" as a hard requirement

These are non-negotiables, not preferences. Write them as regression tests.

The guardrails

Each line is a documented abandonment condition from the files.

Open question the directory raises

"What is the engine refusing to do, and is the refusal deliberate?"

The strategic README flags this as the most actionable item in the set: the typologies converge on it, and no public answer exists. For a planner, it is the question that determines whether the next release is a design decision or a surrender.

Partial answer found in section 11. The live product does document deliberate refusals — zero backend, no install, no render farm, no ffmpeg.wasm. But the same pages add new refusals the persona library never saw, and at least one contradicts a documented persona tolerance.

What the engine does

Most visualizers just react to the spectrum. This one classifies every asset — motion, brightness, hue — and routes each one to the band of the song that suits it. The composition is algorithmic; the score is the audio.

beat detectBass-energy tracker with an adaptive threshold. Locks BPM, fires an envelope every kick, decays gracefully — techno, jazz, and an ill-advised spoken-word record.
RE-MAPAuto-mapper picks from top candidates weighted by score and shuffles blend order. Every click yields a new composition.
exportCanvas captured via MediaRecorder at 30 fps, H.264 + AAC, 8 Mbps. No ffmpeg.wasm, no server, no transcode.
library dropDrop any number of PNG/JPEG/MP4/MP3/WAV. Images pass through an asset curator — chroma-key on a uniform background, then a local tag heuristic. A live chip per file shows the verdict.

The 5 named versions

Single-file variants of the same engine. Same controls, same library, same MP4 export.

The funnel as published

Six releases in eighteen days, then a self-evolving engine.

The published status page is the source of truth for what is live, deprecated and next. It also documents a late change that no persona in the library was written against.

WebGL post-process with 14 FX uniforms (temp, mut, chroma, vignette, grain, sepia, glow, blur, grayscale, posterize…), audio features (bass / mid / treble / air / sub / rms / centroid / beat / onset / bpm), MediaRecorder MP4 export, and project save/load.

v0.1 · initial commit2026-07-31

First commit. Single HTML file, two layers, one shader pass. "The idea is simple: a music video shouldn't require an editor, a renderer, or a render farm."

"We ship new personalities every morning."

This is the single largest addition the persona library never saw. It replaces the fixed preset set the personas were written against with a container that ingests fresh presets daily and mutates a personal variant after three uses.

Read against the strategic set, it is a direct hit on the largest documented anti-signal: five typologies have zero tolerance for popups, notifications or nudges. A daily-arriving gold pill is exactly that mechanism. And read against the live VJ, whose anchor map is a hand-authored set list, a pipeline that ships presets overnight means the map is a moving target.

Resolved as D6 (discovery) and D9 (freeze the pipeline).

11 — Revenue, market & risk

How this is meant to make money, and where the personas push back.

The site publishes a service offer, a licence offer, a five-vector plan, a market study with its own sizing, and a ranked risk list. A planner needs all of it in one place, plus the one thing none of those pages say out loud.

The service tiers

Curated media sets rather than uploaded assets: pick a mood, send your song, get a finished MP4. Five sets are named — Neon Church, Morning Haunt, Brutalist Grid, Dream Sequence, System Error.

add-ons+€10 for 60-second length · +€25 full-song render · +€5 Spotify Canvas crop · +€15 24-hour rush. Bundle: 3+ songs = 15% off any tier. Founding customers (first 5 orders) get 30% off in exchange for portfolio display rights — after that, listed prices apply.

The self-serve licence tiers

Pay once, no subscription. Credit-bank rather than seat-based, justified by irregular release rhythm. Keys arrive by email as swr-{tier}-{code} and are activated via the PT chip in the engine header. Note the fine print — WebM today, MP4 at v1.1. That is D5.

The published willingness-to-pay finding

Van Westendorp PSM across 312 indie creators, May 2026. Indie sweet spot: $8–12/month for unlimited exports, or $0.50–$1.00 per single export pay-as-you-go.

A $5 "remove the watermark" one-time purchase "lands inside the lower edge of the WTP range and tests well." Note this is the finding that makes the hosted tier legible, and the persona refusals that make it dangerous — D8.

Risks the site ranks itself

The thing none of these pages say out loud

Two pages promise "MP4, native, no server" as the core advantage. A third warns that your MIT licence does not cover the bundled media.

The landing page and the campaign page both sell native H.264 + AAC export as the thing that removes ffmpeg, a server and a transcode step. The campaign page's own "read this before you sell" section states that while the source is MIT-licensed, the bundled visual library is not — stock footage, images and clips are not covered — so every asset in a rendered output must be verified for commercial use and redistribution before it is sold. It also notes customers must own or have permission for the audio they submit, and that the docs say modern Chrome or Safari with a wide window, so identical output should not be promised across browsers and devices.

For a planner, that is the top of the dependency list — it sits underneath the entire service tier, the per-video vector and the €55 custom-source tier at once. It is also exactly where the persona library's library-as-metadata finding meets reality: the personas treat the library as tags and metadata, and never once discuss rights.

Resolved as D3 — the highest-priority item in the memo ↓

The stated Belgrade advantage

Three claims the campaign makes as a wedge rather than a constraint: local pricing power (€25 reads as a marketing expense in the EU, a real purchase in Serbia, so the same job wins at roughly double the margin), an authentic Yugo-nostalgia angle (brutalist architecture, CRT scan lines, the warm/cool palette of late-Yugoslav design — "a San Francisco competitor can copy the SVG, they can't copy the intuition"), and in-person closes at KC Grad, Drugstore or Dot.
