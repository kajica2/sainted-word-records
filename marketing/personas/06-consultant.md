# The Consultant

**Slug:** consultant
**One-line:** A working advisor — agency, independent, or in-house — who needs a quick way to point a client at "the kind of artifact we are describing" without installing or pitching a stack.

## Who they are

You are a consultant or senior strategist in your thirties or forties, in an agency, a boutique firm, or an in-house team. You came to Sainted Word Records because a client asked a question you did not want to answer with a slide deck. Your day is a half-day of client calls, half a day of writing or workshop design, and one hour of protecting your calendar. Your toolbelt is Keynote, Miro, a Loom subscription, and a contact list of people who owe you favors.

## What you're trying to do

You want a tool you can hand a client as a reference point — a real, working artifact they can click on, react to, and say "more like this" or "less like this." Success looks like a 30-minute conversation in which a client goes from "explain reactive visuals again" to "send me the link" inside one meeting. You want the artifact to be cheap to share, cheap to ignore, and impossible to misread as a sales pitch.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You have tried to recommend "AI visualizer" SaaS tools to clients and three of them got distracted by the price tag and forgot the conversation. You have tried to recommend a Resolume demo and the client asked when they could buy a license — that's the wrong conversation. Sainted Word Records is free, in the browser, and the landing page does not look like a pitch, which is the rare combination a consultant can defend in front of a CFO without backing up.

## Their first session

1. You drop Sainted Word Records into a Slack thread with a client before a meeting, with a one-line note: "this is the kind of reactivity we are describing."
2. During the meeting, you open the page on a screen-share, drop in a track the client sent last week, and let the engine run without narration for a minute.
3. You switch between two styles to make the point that the engine is not a single look.
4. You stop at the layered library view to point at how the engine treats client-supplied assets.
5. You close the tab before anyone asks about pricing, because that would change the conversation.

## What they'd pay for

You would pay nothing recurring. If Sainted Word Records shipped a "consultant kit" — a downloadable PDF of example use-cases with permission to redraw them for client decks — you would pay once for it, because consultants buy things that save them three meetings a quarter.

## What would make them leave

You would leave forever if the engine ever needed a login before the demo ran, because consultants do not show login-walled tools. You would tolerate a slightly slow first load forever, because you have learned to expect that.

## Quote

"I need an artifact I can show a client without apologizing for it."
