# Film/TV Composer

**Slug:** film-tv-composer
**One-line:** A working composer building a mood reel to land a job, sitting somewhere between Logic and a Director's laptop.

## Who they are

You are 35 to 55, you score indie features and limited series, and you spend half your week chasing the next gig with cold emails and the other half in a project studio with your face in a controller. You heard about Sainted Word Records from a composer friend on a thread about visuals for pitches. Your day is freelance administration, then composing for eight hours, then family, then panic. Your toolbelt is Logic, Pro Tools, a Spitfire library, Kontakt, and an embarrassing folder of Logic projects named `untitled 47`.

## What they're trying to do

You want a one-minute video reel of the best ninety seconds of your last score, cut to picture, that you can attach to a pitch email so the Director does not have to listen to the audio with their eyes closed. Success looks like a video file that lands in their inbox, plays automatically, and conveys the same mood the cue did — atmospheric, tense, hopeful — without you hiring an editor.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You asked an editor to mock-up a reel for your last five pitches and it cost you twelve hundred dollars each time, which is twelve hundred dollars you didn't have. You tried cutting it yourself in iMovie and it looks like iMovie. Sainted Word Records listens to a stems bounce you already exported, picks out the BPM and intensity, and stacks your reference stills (production stills, key art, behind-the-scenes) into a reactive cut that feels like it was edited by someone who knows the scene.

## Their first session

1. You find Sainted Word Records during a Google session that started with "best free reel maker 2026."
2. You upload a 90-second stems bounce and the engine reports BPM 64, key D minor — both correct — and you realize it actually analyzed the audio, which is the moment you relax.
3. You drop in production stills the Director sent you last week and they look strong at full resolution.
4. You pick the `cinematic` or `film` style and the engine applies a letterbox grain that looks more expensive than it should.
5. You export at 1080p and attach it to the next pitch email; the next morning the Director's assistant writes back asking what software you used.

## What they'd pay for

You would pay fifty dollars a year for more than the free quota of high-resolution exports, because you do this four times a year and the math only hurts when you need it now. You would not pay per export because that punishes the moment a Director says "send me one more version with the synth cue up."

## What would make them leave

You would leave forever if exports went above 1080p on free and the page started nudging you to upgrade mid-render — Directors see that and they think you can't afford real software. You would tolerate the missing ProRes export forever, because ffmpeg handles the conversion on your machine in two minutes.

## Quote

"My Director asked me what software made my pitch reel, and I said 'a saxophonist in Belgrade,' and she didn't believe me until she looked it up."
