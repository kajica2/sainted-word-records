# Hip-Hop Beatmaker Going Live on Twitch

**Slug:** twitch-beatmaker
**One-line:** A producer streaming live beat-making sessions to a few hundred regulars, who needs the stream to look busy without a webcam-busy room.

## Who they are

You are 22 to 34, you make trap, drill, or plugg beats, and you stream three to six nights a week on Twitch or YouTube Live from a corner of your apartment with a condenser mic and a backup webcam pointed at your hands. You saw Sainted Word Records linked in a streaming-tools subreddit. Your day is seven hours of producing, two hours of streaming, and one hour answering DMs. Your toolbelt is FL Studio or Ableton, a Stream Deck, OBS, and a Discord server that runs 24/7.

## What they're trying to do

You want your stream to feel like more than a guy in headphones clicking a mouse, because the algorithm punishes static camera angles. Success looks like a layered, audio-reactive graphic that fills the bottom-third of your stream whenever you play back a beat, that pulses with the bass, and that shows the track title, BPM, and key automatically. You want viewers to glance at it and think "he knows what he's doing."

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You paid for a Streamlabs audio visualizer and it looked like a Sci-Fi Channel ident from 2003. You tried building your own in TouchDesigner and it took you six hours to make something that pulses the bass, which was six hours you were not selling beats. Sainted Word Records watches the audio going into OBS and reacts to it, which means your finished beat plays in the player while the visualization reacts below it — and you do not need to wire anything.

## Their first session

1. You find Sainted Word Records on a thread about "OBS audio visualizers" because the existing answers were not good.
2. You pick a beat from your dropbox and upload it; the BPM and key come back correct in about five seconds, which is faster than the beat-detector plugin you already paid thirty dollars for.
3. You import a folder of your own album art (it accepts PNG, JPG, GIF) and the engine starts composing immediately.
4. You hit "no-fade" and pick the `glitch` or `pulse` style because they read clearly on a phone.
5. You point OBS at the Sainted Word Records preview via a window-capture source, which is the moment you almost give up because the window border keeps showing up in your crop.
6. You export a short clip for the channel's "today's beat" highlight reel and post it on Twitter.

## What they'd pay for

You would pay a single ten-dollar one-time fee to remove the few-second delay before the engine starts reacting, because every second of "wait for it…" matters during a live stream. You would not pay any monthly amount because your Twitch income already covers two subscriptions and you cannot add a third.

## What would make them leave

You would leave forever if the visualization ever stalled or dropped frames during a hot moment on stream — your regular viewers would clip it within thirty seconds and you would never hear the end of it on Discord. You would tolerate the missing OBS-native plugin forever because the window-capture workaround mostly works.

## Quote

"My chat kept asking what the moving thing under my hands was, so I told them, and three of them subscribed."
