# Wedding/Bar/Club DJ

**Slug:** working-dj
**One-line:** A solo mobile DJ playing four-to-six hour sets who needs a basic reactive backdrop without learning Resolume.

## Who they are

You are 30 to 52, you DJ weddings, bar mitzvahs, country-club events, and the occasional dive-bar Friday, sometimes solo, sometimes with a partner. You heard about Sainted Word Records from a wedding-vendor Facebook group or from a friend's recommendation. Your day is load gear, set up by 5pm, play until midnight, tear down, invoice, sleep. Your toolbelt is Serato or Rekordbox, a controller, a small PA, and a fog machine you only use when asked.

## What they're trying to do

You want a cheap, reliable visual loop on a projector or TV behind the booth that is not static and not distracting — something a non-DJ audience member will look at once and think, "oh, nice." Success looks like four-to-six hours of "set it and forget it" visuals that match the tempo of whatever track you queue next, without you doing anything between sets except drink water and sign a contract.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You bought a license for ArKaos or Resolume six years ago for a wedding and it sat on your laptop untouched because the learning curve is the size of a flight from JFK to LAX. You tried a few "AI visualizer" SaaS products and they all want a subscription, which is annoying enough to make you give up. Sainted Word Records lets you build a default reactive set in twenty minutes and reuse it at every gig, which is the only math that matters to a solo DJ.

## Their first session

1. You open Sainted Word Records on your laptop the day before a gig, between client emails and an invoice dispute.
2. You drag in twenty of your most-played tracks from last month's weddings, and the engine's browser tab almost dies because you forgot the laptop has 8 GB of RAM and your browser already had forty tabs open — that is the friction.
3. You trim the library down to the ten you actually use, set transitions between songs, and trust the auto-pick.
4. You pick the `glitter` or `aurora` style because it reads as "wedding" without trying.
5. You export the resulting WebM, copy it to a USB drive, plug the drive into the projector at the venue, and run it on a loop — you want a way to make this truly live, but for tonight it works.

## What they'd pay for

You would not pay recurring, because your margins are thin and you already pay for two subs. You would pay a single one-time fee of twenty to fifty dollars for a "venue preset pack" — five different visual packs tuned for wedding/bar/club/everything — that you could license once and reuse. You would not pay for any cloud feature because you already use Dropbox.

## What would make them leave

You would leave forever if Sainted Word Records ever required a login to export — at 11pm at a wedding you have no tolerance for password reset emails. You would tolerate a default six-second delay between tracks because nobody has ever noticed.

## Quote

"I am not going to learn Resolume to play 'Cha Cha Slide' for a roomful of aunts."
