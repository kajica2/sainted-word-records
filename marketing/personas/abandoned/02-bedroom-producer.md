# Bedroom Electronic Producer

**Slug:** bedroom-producer
**One-line:** A lo-fi or hip-hop beatmaker working from a home setup, posting finished tracks and looping visuals to SoundCloud and Bandcamp.

## Who they are

You are 21 to 29, you produce beats on an M-class MacBook with a MIDI keyboard and a pair of headphones, and you release tapes to a small Patreon and a SoundCloud you check obsessively. You found Sainted Word Records through a music-production subreddit or a YouTube production-tutorial tangent. Your day is split between freelance client work, sample hunting, and pushing finished beats. Your toolbelt is Ableton, Splice, a Teenage Engineering PO, and a few hundred drum kits you have collected since you were seventeen.

## What they're trying to do

You want to put a moving image next to every track you upload, because SoundCloud's auto-waveform looks the same on every beat and Bandcamp rewards people who bother. Success looks like a 90-second vertical loop that holds attention on a phone scroll, that you didn't spend more than ten minutes making, that matches the mood of the track without you touching a keyframe. You need it to feel like part of your aesthetic, not an ad for someone else's product.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You tried AE4X, which is a great tool but is sixty bucks and ships with a license manager that pings home. You tried a handful of "AI music visualizer" apps and they all look the same — pulsing circles on a black background, every preset looks like every other preset. Sainted Word Records watches your actual audio file and lets you bring your own palette of clips, which means the visual inherits the same mood you spent an hour dialing in on the master bus.

## Their first session

1. You Google "free audio reactive visualizer no watermark 2026" and click the Sainted Word Records result.
2. The page boots to a black canvas and you almost bounce, because you expected something noisier — you mouse over the stage, find a demo track is auto-loaded, and watch it run.
3. You drop in your WAV, and the engine takes about four seconds to show you a BPM you already know is correct, which earns a small amount of trust.
4. You start dragging in stills from your camera roll — street shots, camera scans, a Polaroid of your friend's cat — the engine accepts MP4s and GIFs but rejects a couple of huge ProRes files because they were dragged straight out of Final Cut.
5. You hit export and wait about ninety seconds; the WebM downloads; you convert it to MP4 in HandBrake with default settings because ffmpeg is for nerds.

## What they'd pay for

You would pay four dollars a month for the marketplace listings you can sell small loop packs through, because right now you sell loops on Gumroad and never make a living at it. Free is enough for the producer's own visual needs. You would pay zero for a subscription that locks existing features behind a paywall.

## What would make them leave

You would leave immediately if Sainted Word Records ever added a watermark to the free export, because watermark removers exist and you have already moved platforms twice over that kind of thing. You would tolerate a 1080p cap on free exports forever, since most of your audience watches on a phone.

## Quote

"I just need a 90-second loop that doesn't look like every other beatmaker's loop, and I need it before I fall asleep tonight."
