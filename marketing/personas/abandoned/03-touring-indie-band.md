# Touring Indie Band Member

**Slug:** touring-indie-band
**One-line:** A guitarist, drummer, or synth player in a four-piece touring indie band that needs stage visuals and has no VJ budget.

## Who they are

You are 26 to 38, you tour nine months a year in a van that smells like damp laundry, and your band has a cult following of three to five thousand people but your merch table barely covers diesel. You saw a Sainted Word Records demo on a Discord you lurk in for indie labels. Your day is load-in, soundcheck, dinner, set, load-out, motel. Your toolbelt is your instrument, in-ear monitors, and the band's shared iPad that the merch person already guards jealously.

## What they're trying to do

You want something projected behind the band during sets that does not look like the screensaver from 2007. Success looks like visuals that move when the room moves — bigger on the loud songs, calmer on the ballads — without you having to hire a friend-of-a-friend to babysit a laptop. You are willing to spend an afternoon building the show before a tour and never touch it again.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You booked a VJ once, and they drank the rider and played the wrong tracks on the wrong songs. You tried making visuals in After Effects and gave up after a week because you are a drummer, not a designer. Sainted Word Records lets you set up a whole show in a single afternoon and hand the iPad to anyone in the band — the engine is dumb-proof enough that your bass player can hand it off without breaking a song.

## Their first session

1. You open `/engine` between soundcheck and dinner, on the promoter's wifi, hoping it doesn't choke.
2. You drag in a USB stick with twenty minutes of live recordings and the engine stalls for ten seconds because the stick is USB 2.0 — this is the moment you almost give up.
3. Once it loads, you pick a `psychedelic` or `smoke` style from the versions panel because those feel least dated.
4. You import a few hundred photos from the band's Google Drive, mostly live shots and Polaroids of the van, and the engine stacks them into layers you can reorder.
5. You select what the band calls "the rippers" — six of the loudest live recordings — and skip the calm ones, because you'll play the calm ones to set up and not project visuals then.
6. You export and copy the WebM to a USB stick at the merch table, hand the iPad to whoever is sober, and walk on stage.

## What they'd pay for

You would pay forty dollars for a one-time "show mode" unlock that listens to a click track from your drum monitor instead of pre-baked audio, so the visuals sync live to whatever tempo you play. Anything subscription-based is out because the band treasurer would say no.

## What would make them leave

You would leave forever if the visual ever drifted noticeably out of beat — bands are unforgiving about that, and the audio engineer in the crowd will mutter something on the way to the bar. You would tolerate a four-percent CPU drain on the iPad, since the band already owns three adapters.

## Quote

"We paid a VJ three hundred dollars last tour to play the wrong song during our last song."
