# Busking Jazz Saxophonist

**Slug:** busking-saxophonist
**One-line:** A working jazz sax player on a sidewalk or in a transit hall, performing solo and needing moving visuals to stop foot traffic.

## Who they are

You are 32 to 48, trained in conservatory or by the road, and you play three to six hours of solo saxophone sets in subway stations, festival courtyards, and cobblestone squares across Europe. You heard about Sainted Word Records from a fellow busker in Krakow or a video someone reposted on Instagram. Your day is travel, soundcheck, gig, tip jar, dinner, sleep. Your toolbelt is your horn, a portable amp, a small mixer, and the phone in your pocket.

## What they're trying to do

You need a moving visual behind you when you play, because still ground performs worse than reactive ground. Success looks like a passive viewer pausing for forty seconds, then another, then a small crowd, then enough tips to make rent. You want to point a tablet at the wall behind you, drop in a live recording or a loop, and have it breathe with your playing without you touching anything for an hour.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You tried Resolume on a laptop, but it's a six-hundred-dollar VJ app that needs a separate audio interface and someone to tap spacebar at the bridge. You tried setting up a projector with a slow-shimmer MP4 loop, but a static loop gets ignored by the third passerby. Sainted Word Records listens to whatever your horn is pushing through the amp and pushes visuals that respond, which means a quiet ballad breathes differently than a Coltrane changes run, and you do not have to touch anything between songs.

## Their first session

1. You open `/engine` on a free evening, before a gig, with coffee.
2. You upload a track you already recorded — the engine reports a BPM and key you don't quite trust because it's a free Reed player site, so you play ten seconds and watch the visualizations shift.
3. You realize the engine needs you to drop in your own clips, and you have almost nothing on your phone — you screenshot a few album covers and a photo of the venue, drop them in, and watch it come together.
4. You hit export and a WebM lands in your downloads, which is the part that almost stops you because your phone wants MP4 and you don't have ffmpeg installed.
5. You wire the tablet to a cheap Bluetooth speaker's headphone-out, you set it on a music stand behind you during the next gig, and you leave it playing the WebM between songs — you wish there was a way to use the mic input live instead of an audio file.

## What they'd pay for

You would pay twenty euros, once, for a "live mic mode" that listens to whatever your horn is putting out in real time and reacts to it. Free is fine for the recorded-export loop. You would pay nothing for a monthly subscription because busker economics don't allow it.

## What would make them leave

You would never come back if the engine lost its free, no-signup, browser-native promise — buskers cannot install native apps on borrowed tablets and they cannot remember passwords. You would tolerate the missing live-mic mode forever and just use the pre-exported clips on busy days.

## Quote

"If I have to learn another piece of software before Saturday's gig in the Old Town, I'm going to quit sax and sell insurance."
