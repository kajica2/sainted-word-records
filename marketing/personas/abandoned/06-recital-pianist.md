# Classical Pianist at a Recital

**Slug:** recital-pianist
**One-line:** A conservatory-trained pianist doing a Scriabin or Ravel recital who wants a single screen behind the piano, no DJ.

## Who they are

You are 28 to 60, you give solo piano recitals in regional concert halls and chamber-music festivals, and your program is the standard repertoire plus one contemporary piece per set. You heard about Sainted Word Records from a colleague at a conservatory or a piece on a pianist's forum. Your day is practice, teaching, family, and the small administrative task of recital prep that nobody talks about. Your toolbelt is your Steinway or Bösendorff, a tablet or laptop for sheet music, and a polite aversion to laptops on stage.

## What they're trying to do

You want a single screen behind the piano that reacts to whatever you are playing, the way a few famous pianists have done — not flashy, not abstract fireworks, but a mood: watercolors for Ravel, embers for Scriabin, architecture for Bach. Success looks like a recital that the local paper mentions in two sentences as "accompanied by live reactive visuals," not a recital that needs an AV tech.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You asked the concert hall's AV tech to set up a projector and a Resolume rig for your last recital, and it cost the presenter twelve hundred dollars, which they said they would not do again. You tried opening iTunes Visualizer in the green room and you were embarrassed for yourself. Sainted Word Records lets you pick a style that matches each piece on the program, switch between them with a tap, and not touch anything for the rest of the recital.

## Their first session

1. You open Sainted Word Records two weeks before the recital, in your living room, with the windows closed because neighbors.
2. You upload a recording of yourself playing Ravel's `Ondine` and the engine reports BPM 84, which is correct, and a key of E-flat minor-ish, which is close enough that you trust it.
3. You drop in photos you took in the French countryside last summer, which is what the program note is about.
4. You pick the `watercolor` style because it is the least "rave" of the options.
5. You practice once with the visuals running on your iPad propped against the music stand — the moment of friction is that you realize the engine does not listen to your microphone, only to a file.
6. You hire one responsible student to babysit the iPad during the recital and pay them in coffee and pastries.

## What they'd pay for

You would pay one hundred dollars, once, for a "listening mode" that picks up a microphone signal and does not require a mixer. Recital budgets are real, but one hundred dollars is cheaper than the AV tech. Anything subscription-based feels wrong for classical music, which is the genre that still pays annual dues.

## What would make them leave

You would leave forever if a single visual ever strobed in a way that could trigger a photosensitive audience member — concert halls have older audiences, and even one seizure at your recital ends your reputation. You would tolerate a 720p export because recital halls are not 4K.

## Quote

"I would like one screen behind the piano that does not scream 'electronic music,' and I do not want to learn TouchDesigner to get it."
