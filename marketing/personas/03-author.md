# The Author

**Slug:** author
**One-line:** A writer of books — fiction, essays, long-form journalism — who treats audio-reactive video as a new paragraph in an evolving medium rather than a way to illustrate a song.

## Who they are

You are a published author in your forties or fifties — novels or nonfiction, with a serious readership and a small but consistent publication pipeline. You came across Sainted Word Records by way of a friend who works in publishing and who flagged it not for what it does, but for what it implies. Your day is morning pages, revision in the afternoon, and correspondence with editors and agents. Your toolbelt is Scrivener, a stack of unread galleys, a notebook, and the kind of patience that does not translate to SaaS tools.

## What they're trying to do

You are not here to ship a music video. You want to know whether a written idea can be expressed as an audio-reactive composition — and you want to test whether a paragraph in a book can become a multi-sensory object without losing its center of gravity. Success looks like a single short-form artifact — maybe twelve seconds — that a reader can read on a phone, that carries one image of your prose to one piece of music, and that you can defend as an extension of the work rather than a marketing stunt.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You have been pitched "audiobook companion apps" and they all turn the book into a checklist. You have been pitched "AI illustration" tools and they all turn the book into a slideshow. Sainted Word Records takes an audio file and reacts to it, which means the same paragraph written in two different moods — say, a quiet one and a cynical one — produces two visually different objects, and that subtle distinction is what interests you as a writer.

## Their first session

1. You find Sainted Word Records during a slow afternoon and read the `about` page first.
2. You choose an MP3 of a piece of music you have listened to for years and open the page, expecting nothing.
3. You drag in a single photograph from your desk — your editor's handwriting, a coffee cup, a window — and watch the engine make the photograph breathe.
4. You realize the page is not a video editor, it is a sensor. You write three sentences into a notebook about what that means.
5. You bookmark the page. You do not export yet. You want to sit with the idea for a week.

## What they'd pay for

You would pay almost nothing in cash; you would, however, pay attention. If Sainted Word Records eventually shipped a "paperback" mode — something that sent a still image and a track to a printer — you would pay for that once, for the same reason authors pay for offset printing. Anything subscription-based would not make sense for a working author.

## What would make them leave

You would leave forever if the engine ever framed itself as a "content creation tool," because that phrase turns authors into audiences. You would tolerate some bugs forever, because books ship with errata.

## Quote

"I came for the music and stayed for the implication."
