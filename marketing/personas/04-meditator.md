# The Meditator

**Slug:** meditator
**One-line:** A long-time practitioner of meditation — Vipassana, zazen, or a contemporary lineage — who reads reactive video as a perceptual instrument rather than a relaxation product.

## Who they are

You are a practitioner in your forties or fifties with a steady practice, possibly a teacher in a tradition that does not use words like "mindfulness" lightly. You came across Sainted Word Records because a meditation-app founder mentioned it in a conversation about what counts as a "bell." Your day is sitting, walking, family, work, sitting again. Your toolbelt is your cushion, your timer, a sangha you trust, and a quiet suspicion of any screen-based tool that claims to help.

## What they're trying to do

You want to understand what an audio-reactive video looks like from inside attention — what it does to perception, what it resists, what it amplifies. Success looks like a forty-minute sitting in which the engine runs quietly and reveals nothing new, which is exactly what you wanted: confirmation that the screen is transparent to the practice. You are not looking for content; you are looking for a particular kind of silence.

## Why they don't use the alternatives

You have tried meditation apps and they are apps. You have tried ambient-video tools and they are wallpaper. Sainted Word Records listens and reacts, which means it is closer to a mirror than a soundtrack, and you want to know whether a mirror that responds to your own breath is a useful object or a dangerous one.

## Their first session

1. You open the page on a laptop you keep for non-practice use, after the morning sit, with the room quiet.
2. You let the page sit for a minute without touching it, to feel the default temperature of the tool.
3. You load a single track — a bell, then silence — and you notice that the engine treats silence as silence, which is the only thing you needed to confirm.
4. You run the engine through a slow visual style for ten minutes while you sit on the floor nearby, eyes open or closed.
5. You write one paragraph in your practice journal about whether the screen supports or interferes, which is the question you came to answer.

## What they'd pay for

You would not pay for a meditation product — that is a category you have heard about and declined. If Sainted Word Records eventually shipped an offline, no-telemetry version for retreat centers, you would ask two friends in retreat leadership to evaluate it.

## What would make them leave

You would leave forever if a single update introduced a pop-up, a notification, or any kind of nudge — the engine has to remain as quiet as the room it interrupts. You would tolerate a small audio glitch at the start of every load forever, because every meditation tool has an edge.

## Quote

"A tool that requires silence in order to be heard is rare, and I will not be the one to break it."
