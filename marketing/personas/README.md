# Strategic Personas — README

These are not consumer personas of Sainted Word Records. They are **strategic-collaborator typologies** — eleven roles the engine's creator (a working jazz saxophonist in Belgrade) uses, or could use, to think about his own work at the meta level.

The original brief asked for a granular consumer-persona library covering every plausible end-user of the engine (buskers, beatmakers, real-estate agents, AR business-card designers, and so on). That work was started as a separate consumer-persona library (eight files) and then cancelled at the user's request before completion. The cancelled files are not deleted — they remain on disk under `marketing/personas/abandoned/` as a record of the abandoned track — but they are **explicitly not part of this deliverable**.

## What this set is for

Each of the eleven files in this directory is a typology of someone the engine's creator would *think with*, not someone he would *market to*. They are written in second-person, with the engine's creator as the implied reader, so a phrase like "You want to know whether the engine is a real example of the pattern you study" reads as the creator talking to himself as the typology.

The eleven roles were specified by the engine's creator from his own life and working network. They are not derived from market research; they are the cast of characters he chooses to model for.

## How to read these files

The template is the same one used in consumer-persona work — Who they are / What they're trying to do / Why they don't use the alternatives / First session / What they'd pay for / What would make them leave / Quote — but every section is reframed around **how this typology would push the engine's creator's work forward**, not what they would click in the engine.

These are tools for thinking, not targets for marketing.

## What we noticed

- The eleven typologies cluster into three implicit modes: the **Auditor** (designer, philosopher, multisystem specialist) reads the engine to find what it is and is not; the **Practitioner** (music tutor, meditator, author) reads the engine to find what it does to attention over time; the **Broker** (consultant, system designer) reads the engine to position it for other people.
- Three of the eleven — the designer, the philosopher, the multisystem specialist — want the engine to remain **unfinished and legible**. They leave forever the moment the engine starts hiding its seams. This is a real constraint on any future redesign that smooths the surface.
- Two of the eleven — the meditator and the author — are explicitly **anti-distraction**: their tolerance for popups, notifications, and nudges is zero. This is a small set but a strong signal about who Sainted Word Records *should not* try to please.
- The consultant typology is the only one whose deliverable is **a forwarded URL**. Everything else in the directory is in service of making the URL safe to forward in a high-stakes room.
- The author and the philosopher both do not export in their first session. The artifact is not the point; the **idea the artifact makes legible** is the point. That is unusual for a tool that has "render" in its UI.
- No typology in this set asks for a feature that the marketing site already advertises. Every "what they would pay for" is something **the engine has not yet shipped**. That is the point — these are forward-looking collaborators, not users.
- The typologies converge on a question the engine's creator has not publicly answered: **what is the engine refusing to do, and is the refusal deliberate?** That question is the most actionable thing in this directory.
- Three of eleven typologies — designer, philosopher, system designer — would each, in their own register, push back on a future "platform" relaunch. That convergence is worth flagging as a guardrail, not a slogan.
