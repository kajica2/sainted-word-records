# Section I — The Crisis Protocol

**The Emergency Anchor**

Use this when the floor falls out. Forget the "steps" and focus on survival.

---

## 1. The Immediate Stabilization (0–24 Hours)

### The 3-3-3 Rule
Name 3 things you see, 3 sounds you hear, and move 3 parts of your body.

This interrupts the spiral of catastrophic thinking by forcing the nervous system into the present moment. Sight, sound, and proprioception — the three most reliable channels back to *here*.

### Box Breathing
Inhale 4 seconds. Hold 4 seconds. Exhale 4 seconds. Hold 4 seconds.

This is a biological kill switch for panic. The extended exhale activates the vagus nerve and tells the parasympathetic system it is safe to stand down. Practice it now, while you are calm, so your body knows the pattern when you need it most.

### Lower the Bar
Survival is the only metric of success.

Not productivity. Not insight. Not gratitude. Not "becoming a better person through this." Just: stay alive, stay breathing, stay in this hour. The rest of the protocol depends on this.

---

## 2. The Sustainment (Days 2–14)

### Labeling
Say *"There is pain"* rather than *"I am pain."*

The grammatical shift from "I am" to "there is" creates distance between the observer and the experience. You are not the pain. You are the one who sees the pain. This is not denial — it is precision.

### The One-Hour Window
Do not look at next month. Only look at the next 60 minutes.

The mind in crisis tries to solve the whole problem at once and collapses under the weight of the unsolvable. The one-hour window is an act of mercy toward your future self. Next month will exist next month. This hour exists now.

### Bilateral Movement
Walk. The left-right rhythm helps the brain process trauma.

Walking is not a metaphor. Alternating left-right movement stimulates both hemispheres of the brain in coordination, which is what the nervous system needs in order to integrate overwhelming experience. You do not need to walk far. You do not need to walk fast. You need to walk.

---

## When this section is not enough

The Crisis Protocol is a survival kit, not a treatment plan. If you are in sustained crisis — weeks of unremitting darkness, intrusive thoughts, inability to function — please seek a professional. The Atlas is a complement to that work, not a substitute.

You are not supposed to do this alone.
